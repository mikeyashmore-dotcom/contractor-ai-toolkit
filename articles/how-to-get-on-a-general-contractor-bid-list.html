How to Get on a General Contractor's Bid List

More bid invitations = more work. Here's how subs get on the list.

If you're a subcontractor, steady work comes down to one thing: being on the bid lists of GCs who build in your area. The good news — most of them are always looking for reliable subs. You just have to reach out the right way and be easy to work with.

Step 1 — Make a target list

Find the GCs building the kind of work you do in your area. Sources: local permit records, construction news, supply houses, plan rooms (like your local Builders Exchange or online plan services), and simple word of mouth.

Step 2 — Reach out short and simple

GCs are busy. A one-paragraph intro beats a brochure. Here's a template:

Hi [Name], I run [Company], a [trade] subcontractor working in [area]. I'd love to be added to your bid list for upcoming projects. Happy to send our license, insurance, and a few references — just let me know the best way. Thanks for considering us.

Step 3 — Have your paperwork ready

When they say "send your info," respond fast with: license number, proof of insurance, a short list of comparable projects, and references. Being organized here signals you'll be organized on the job.

Step 4 — Respond to every invitation

Once you're on a list, the fastest way to get kept on it — and invited to more — is to respond to every bid invitation, even the ones you can't take. A quick "can't fit this one, but keep me in mind for the next" keeps you in good standing. Ghosting a GC gets you quietly dropped.

Step 5 — Follow up

After you bid, follow up politely on a schedule. The sub who checks in and answers questions wins the jobs the silent bidders lose.

Frequently asked questions

How many GCs should I reach out to?

As many as build your type of work in your area — the more lists you're on, the steadier your pipeline. Aim to keep adding a few every month.

What if I'm brand new with no references?

Lead with your license, insurance, and any related experience, and be upfront. Many GCs will give a reliable new sub a small job to prove out. Reliability beats résumé.
