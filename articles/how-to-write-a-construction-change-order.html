How to Write a Construction Change Order (+ Free Template)

A plain-English guide for contractors and estimators — any trade.

Few things cost contractors more money than doing extra work without getting it in writing. The job grows, you handle it to keep things moving, and when the invoice goes out the customer balks at the number. A change order is how you avoid that — and writing one takes about five minutes.

What is a change order?

A change order is a short, signed document that modifies your original contract or proposal. It records three things: what changed in the scope of work, what changed in the price, and what changed in the schedule. Once both parties sign, it becomes part of the contract.

When should you use one?

Any time the work differs from what you signed for:

  • You find a hidden condition on site (more area, rot, an obstruction the plans didn't show)
  • The owner or GC asks for something extra
  • A material gets substituted for a different price
  • The timeline shifts because of a change

Rule of thumb: if it changes the price or the schedule, it needs a change order.

What to include

  1. Change order number and the original proposal/contract number
  2. Project and customer details
  3. Reason for the change — plain description of what changed and why
  4. Change in work — what's being added or removed
  5. Change in price — original total, this change (+/–), and the new total
  6. Change in schedule — added days or a new completion date
  7. Signature lines for both you and the customer
The one line that protects you: put in bold that "work will not begin until this change order is signed." Getting the signature before the extra work — not after — is the whole point.

Let AI write the first draft

The wording is the part people stall on. You can hand the facts to a chat AI (ChatGPT or Claude) and have it draft the "reason for change" and customer message in seconds. A prompt like:

"Write the 'reason for change' section of a construction change order. What happened: [we found 400 extra sq ft not shown on the plans that needs the same scope]. Added cost [$X]. Keep it clear and professional, and note that I need written approval before proceeding."

You still fill in the numbers and check it — AI is bad at math — but you start from a draft instead of a blank page.

Frequently asked questions

Does a change order have to be signed by both parties?

Yes. It's only enforceable when both the contractor and the customer sign — and it should be signed before the work is done.

Is a verbal change order valid?

Rarely worth the risk. Even a quick text or email confirming the scope and price beats a handshake you can't prove later. Get it in writing.

What if the customer refuses to sign?

Then you don't do the extra work. That's exactly what the change order protects you from — doing unpaid work you can't collect on.
