Look professional and protect yourself — for any trade.

A good proposal wins the job and keeps you out of trouble. A vague one gets you a "we went with someone else," or worse, an argument at invoice time. Here's exactly what to include, plus a free template you can copy.

What every construction proposal should include

  1. Your company info — name, address, phone, email, license number
  2. Proposal number and dates — including a "valid until" date
  3. Customer and project — who and where
  4. Scope of work — a clear, itemized list of what you'll do
  5. Exclusions — what's NOT included (this is the part most people skip and regret)
  6. Price — the number, and whether tax is included
  7. Payment terms — deposit, progress payments, balance due
  8. Signature line — acceptance turns it into an agreement
The two lines that save you money: a clear exclusions list and a "valid for 30 days" date. Exclusions stop scope creep; the expiry date protects you from a customer accepting a six-month-old price after material costs jumped.

Frequently asked questions

Is a proposal legally binding?

Once both parties sign and there's agreed consideration, a proposal generally becomes a binding contract. Have a professional review your terms if the jobs are large.

How long should a proposal be?

As short as it can be while still covering scope, exclusions, price, and terms. One clear page beats five vague ones.
